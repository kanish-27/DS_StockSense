# STOCKSENSE Synthetic Dataset

> ⚠️ **This dataset is SYNTHETIC.** It was generated by
> `src/data/generate_synthetic_data.py` from hand-written rules plus random noise.
> Use it to build, train and test the pipeline — not to draw real business
> conclusions. Store names, brands and suppliers are fictional; holiday dates are
> approximate.

**Regenerate (from the project root):**
```bash
python -m src.data.generate_synthetic_data                      # default, seed 42
python -m src.data.generate_synthetic_data --no-quality-issues  # clean version
```
The same seed always produces the same files.

## Overview

| File | Rows | Grain | Key |
|------|-----:|-------|-----|
| `stores.csv` | 10 | one row per store | `store_id` |
| `products.csv` | 40 | one row per product | `product_id` |
| `external_factors.csv` | 2,924 | one row per region per day | `date` + `region` |
| `inventory.csv` | 279,242 | one row per store × product × day | `date` + `store_id` + `product_id` |
| `transactions.csv` | 265,260 | daily aggregated sales per store × product (only days with sales > 0) | `transaction_id` |

- **Period:** 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (731 days)
- **Stores:** 10 across 4 regions (South, West, North, East); formats Hypermarket / Supermarket / Express
- **Products:** 40 across 8 categories. Express stores carry 34 (no Frozen, no 5 kg staples, no candles).
- **Currency:** INR

## Relationships

```
stores.store_id ──┬── inventory.store_id
                  └── transactions.store_id
stores.region ─────── external_factors.region   (join on region + date)
products.product_id ┬─ inventory.product_id
                    └─ transactions.product_id
inventory (date, store_id, product_id) ── transactions (date, store_id, product_id)
```

## Things to know for modeling

- **Sales are censored by stock.** On stock-out days `units_sold` is capped at the
  stock available, so it can be lower than true demand. True demand is not
  included, as in real retail data.
- **Days with zero sales have no row in `transactions.csv`.** Use `inventory.csv`
  for the complete store × product × day grid.
- **Price revision:** every product's price was revised (0–8% increase) on
  2025-04-01, so `unit_price_inr` can differ from `base_price_inr`.
- **What drives demand in the simulation:** weekday pattern (weekends are higher),
  gradual growth over time, store size, temperature (summer/winter items),
  rainfall (monsoon items; heavy rain slightly lowers footfall), festivals
  (especially Diwali), promotions, local events, and random noise.
- **Why stock-outs happen:** reorder points are fixed and do not adapt to seasonal
  peaks, festivals or promotions; suppliers are sometimes late (1–4 days) or deliver
  only part of an order. About 4.3% of store × product × days end with zero stock.

## Deliberate data-quality issues

These were injected on purpose so the cleaning step has realistic work. They are
absent when generated with `--no-quality-issues`.

| stores.csv | Inconsistent city text for S002 (`" chennai"` — leading space, lowercase) | 1 row |
